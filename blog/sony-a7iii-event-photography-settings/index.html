Camera Settings

Sony A7III Event Photography Settings: A Cheat Sheet You Can Read On Location

Exact Sony A7III settings for receptions, ceremonies, conferences, and dim ballrooms, plus the menu setup that keeps you from missing frames.

By Joshua Smith
June 29, 2026
6 min read

In this guide

  1. Set the camera up once, before you leave
  2. Settings by lighting condition
  3. How to meter for darker skin tones
  4. The three things that cost people frames
  5. FAQ

Most event photography advice tells you to shoot in manual and figure it out. That is useless when the lights drop for a first dance and you have four seconds.

These are the settings I run at paid events, organized by the lighting condition you are standing in.

Set the camera up once, before you leave

Do this at home. Never do it in a venue.

  • File format: RAW, uncompressed or lossless compressed. Never JPEG only. You cannot recover a blown highlight or fix mixed tungsten and LED white balance from a JPEG.
  • Drive mode: Continuous Low. Continuous High fills the buffer and gives you six nearly identical frames of the same blink.
  • Focus mode: AF-C.
  • Focus area: Wide, with Face and Eye Priority on. Switch to Zone when the room is crowded and the camera keeps grabbing the wrong face.
  • Metering: Multi, with Face Priority in Multi Metering turned on.
  • Custom button: Map Eye AF to AF-ON. Map ISO to the rear dial.
  • Silent shooting: On for ceremonies and speeches. Off for anything with movement or LED lighting, because rolling shutter and banding will ruin frames.
  • Two cards: Slot 1 RAW, Slot 2 simultaneous backup. This is not optional for paid work.

Settings by lighting condition

Indoor reception, bounce flash available

SettingValue
ModeManual
Aperturef/2.8
Shutter1/200
ISO1600 to 3200
FlashTTL, bounce off ceiling or wall behind you, 0 to -0.3 EV
White balanceFlash or 5600K

Keep ISO high enough that the room still reads in the background. If you drop to ISO 400 and let the flash do everything, you get a black cave behind every subject. The whole point of raising ISO is to keep the venue alive.

Ceremony or speech, no flash allowed

SettingValue
ModeAperture priority or manual with Auto ISO
Aperturef/2.8
Shutter1/250 minimum
ISO3200 to 12800, capped at 12800
Silent shutterOn
White balanceAuto, fixed after a test frame

The A7III holds up to ISO 12800 better than most people expect. Noise is fixable in post. Motion blur is not.

Dim ballroom, dancing, moving lights

SettingValue
Aperturef/1.8 with the 50mm, f/2.8 with the zoom
Shutter1/160 to 1/200
ISO3200 to 6400
FlashTTL bounce, -0.3 to -0.7 EV
FocusAF-C, Zone, Eye AF on the AF-ON button

Drag the shutter slightly to catch the color from the DJ lights. Below 1/125 you start losing hands and faces to blur.

Conference or panel, mixed room lighting

SettingValue
Aperturef/2.8 to f/4
Shutter1/250
ISO1600 to 6400
White balanceCustom, set from a white sheet of paper under the stage lights

Conference rooms are the worst white balance environments you will shoot. Tungsten downlights, LED panels, and a projector all hitting the same face. Set a custom white balance for the stage and a second one for the hallway, and shoot RAW so you can split the difference later.

Outdoor, golden hour portraits during an event

SettingValue
Aperturef/2 to f/2.8
Shutter1/500 to 1/2000
ISO100 to 400
FlashTTL fill at -1.0 to -1.3 EV, HSS on
MeteringSpot on the face

How to meter for darker skin tones

Multi metering pulls toward the whole scene, and in a bright room it will underexpose a dark-skinned subject to protect a window or a white shirt.

Two fixes that work.

Meter off the face. Switch to spot metering linked to the focus point and place it on the cheek. Then push exposure compensation up by roughly a third to two thirds of a stop.

Watch the histogram, not the screen. In a dark venue your screen looks brighter than the file. Expose so skin lands in the upper middle of the histogram, not the left third. Recovering shadows on dark skin introduces color noise fast, and it shifts tones green.

Getting exposure right on set saves you an hour of correction per gallery. For the editing side of this, read Editing for Black Skin Tones in Lightroom.

The three things that cost people frames

  1. Shutter too slow. 1/60 with IBIS is fine for a still room and useless for people. Floor at 1/160.
  2. ISO too low. Underexposing to keep the file clean produces a noisier file after you lift it in post than shooting brighter at a higher ISO.
  3. Continuous High. Fills the buffer, misses the moment after the moment.

For which lens to put on the body, read Tamron 28-75 G2 vs A036. For flash technique outdoors, read Flash as Fill in Harsh Midday Sun.

FAQ

What ISO is too high on a Sony A7III?

Files stay usable to ISO 12800 with modern noise reduction. Past 12800 you lose color detail in shadows, which hurts skin tones first.

Should I shoot events in manual or aperture priority?

Manual with a fixed ISO for controlled light. Manual with Auto ISO and a minimum shutter for a room where the light changes every few minutes.

What shutter speed do I need for dancing?

1/200 freezes most movement. 1/160 works if you want a little motion in hands and hair. Below 1/125 you start throwing away frames.

Do I need silent shutter for ceremonies?

It helps in quiet rooms. Turn it off under LED stage lighting because the electronic shutter produces banding.

How do I stop mixed lighting from ruining skin tones?

Shoot RAW, set a custom white balance in the main room, and gel your flash to match the ambient when tungsten dominates. A CTO gel on the flash solves most of it.

How many cards should I bring to an event?

Enough to never swap mid-event, and always write to both slots. A single card is the cheapest way to lose a paid job.
