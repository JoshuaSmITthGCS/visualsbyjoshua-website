The Backup Gear Checklist: What Fails at Paid Events and What to Carry

Use this failure-first event photography gear checklist to prepare backup flashes, cards, batteries, cameras, lenses, cables, and chargers.

My Godox V1 died between one shot and the next. No warning, no gradual decline. The flash simply stopped firing and the rest of the coverage happened on ambient light and a raised ISO.

That is the correct way to think about gear. Not what is best, but what happens when this specific item stops working thirty minutes into a job someone paid for.

What actually fails, ranked

Based on my own failures and what every working photographer reports.

  1. Flashes. By a wide margin. Contacts wear, batteries swell, hot shoes crack, internal capacitors give out.
  2. Batteries. Cold rooms and long events. A battery at 40 percent in a cold venue reads 0 an hour later.
  3. Memory cards. Rare but catastrophic. Corruption usually appears during the ingest, not during the shoot.
  4. Cables and chargers. Left at home, or the one that lives in the bag frays.
  5. Lenses. Least likely, most expensive. Usually AF motor failure, and usually preceded by warning signs.
  6. Bodies. Rarely fail outright. They fail by getting dropped.

Notice the order. Most photographers buy a second body first and carry one flash. That is backwards.

The dual card slot on the A7III is the most important backup you own. Set slot 2 to simultaneous recording and never turn it off. A corrupted card stops being a lost job and becomes an inconvenience.

The night before checklist

Run this every time. Do not trust memory.

  • ☐ Format both cards in camera, not on a computer
  • ☐ Charge every battery, camera and flash
  • ☐ Confirm slot 2 is set to simultaneous RAW recording
  • ☐ Confirm file format is RAW, not JPEG
  • ☐ Set date, time, and copyright metadata
  • ☐ Clean the sensor and both lens elements
  • ☐ Fire the flash 20 times at full power to check recycle time
  • ☐ Test the trigger with each flash on each channel
  • ☐ Pack the spare trigger, spare cards, spare batteries
  • ☐ Confirm the address, contact name, and arrival time in writing
  • ☐ Check the venue's power and lighting situation
  • ☐ Screenshot the shot list to your phone

The bag

Beyond the gear itself.

  • Lens cloth and a rocket blower
  • Gaffer tape
  • Business cards
  • A rain sleeve, or a plastic bag if you are in Charleston in August
  • Snacks and water for anything over three hours
  • Portable charger for your phone
  • Small first aid items, mainly bandages for shoes clients did not break in

When you cannot afford a backup

Renting is cheaper than failing. A second body for a Saturday costs $60 to $90 in either Charleston or the DMV. That is a fraction of an event fee and it removes the worst-case outcome entirely.

If renting is not an option, adjust what you promise. Do not book a wedding on one body. Do book a two hour corporate event on one body and one flash, and carry a second flash because that is the part likely to break.

What goes in the contract

Language protects you when gear fails despite the backups.

  • A clause covering equipment failure and what remedy the client receives
  • A clause covering weather and rescheduling
  • A defined delivery window, not a promise of a specific date
  • A statement that you deliver edited images and retain RAW files

The post-event routine

The job is not done when you leave.

  1. Ingest both cards to two separate drives before you sleep.
  2. Verify the file count matches the shot count on camera.
  3. Do not format the cards until the gallery is delivered and backed up in a third location.
  4. Log which gear misbehaved. Patterns show up over a season.

FAQ

Do I need two camera bodies for event photography?

For weddings and full day coverage, yes. For a two hour session or a short corporate event, one body with dual cards and two flashes covers the realistic failure modes.

What is the most common gear failure at events?

Flash failure, followed by batteries running out earlier than expected in cold rooms.

Should I use both memory card slots?

Always, set to simultaneous recording. It is free insurance against the single failure that ends a job with no recovery.

How many batteries do I need for a four hour event?

Four camera batteries and two flash batteries minimum. Continuous shooting with the rear screen and flash modeling lamp drains faster than the rated numbers suggest.

Is renting backup gear worth it?

Yes for anything you cannot reshoot. A rental fee is a small percentage of an event rate and it removes the risk of delivering nothing.

When should I format my memory cards?

Only after the gallery is delivered and backed up in at least two places, and only in camera.
