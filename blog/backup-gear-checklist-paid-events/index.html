The Backup Gear Checklist: What Fails at Paid Events and What to Carry

Use this failure-first event photography gear checklist to prepare backup flashes, cards, batteries, cameras, lenses, cables, and chargers.

By Joshua Smith
July 21, 2026
6 min read

My Godox V1 died between one shot and the next. No warning, no gradual decline. It just stopped firing, and I finished the rest of the coverage on ambient light and a raised ISO.

That's how you should think about gear: not what's best, but what happens when this specific item quits thirty minutes into a job someone paid for.

What actually fails, ranked

This ranking comes from my own failures and from what other working photographers report.

  1. Flashes. By a wide margin. Contacts wear, batteries swell, hot shoes crack, internal capacitors give out.
  2. Batteries. Cold rooms and long events drain them fast. A battery reading 40 percent in a cold venue reads 0 an hour later.
  3. Memory cards. Rare but catastrophic. Corruption usually shows up during ingest, not during the shoot.
  4. Cables and chargers. You leave one at home, or the one that lives in the bag frays.
  5. Lenses. Least likely, most expensive. Usually an AF motor failure, and usually preceded by warning signs.
  6. Bodies. They rarely fail outright. They fail by getting dropped.

Notice the order. Most photographers buy a second body first and carry one flash. That's backwards.

The dual card slot on the Sony A7III is the most important backup you own. Set slot 2 to simultaneous recording and leave it on. A corrupted card stops being a lost job and becomes an inconvenience.

Getting ready: checklist and bag

The night-before checklist

Run this every time. Don't trust memory.

  • ☐ Format both cards in camera, not on a computer
  • ☐ Charge every battery, camera and flash
  • ☐ Confirm slot 2 is set to simultaneous RAW recording
  • ☐ Confirm file format is RAW, not JPEG
  • ☐ Set date, time, and copyright metadata
  • ☐ Clean the sensor and both lens elements
  • ☐ Fire the flash 20 times at full power to check recycle time
  • ☐ Test the trigger with each flash on each channel
  • ☐ Pack the spare trigger, spare cards, spare batteries
  • ☐ Confirm the address, contact name, and arrival time in writing
  • ☐ Check the venue's power and lighting situation
  • ☐ Screenshot the shot list to your phone

What's in the bag

Beyond the gear itself, I carry:

  • Lens cloth and a rocket blower
  • Gaffer tape
  • Business cards
  • A rain sleeve, or a plastic bag if you're in Charleston in August
  • Snacks and water for anything over three hours
  • Portable charger for your phone
  • Small first aid items, mainly bandages for shoes clients didn't break in

Protecting the job: rentals and contracts

Renting is cheaper than failing. A second body for a Saturday costs $60 to $90 in either Charleston or the DMV. That's a fraction of an event fee, and it removes the worst-case outcome entirely.

If renting isn't an option, adjust what you promise. Don't book a wedding on one body. You can book a two-hour corporate event on one body and one flash, but carry a second flash, since that's the part most likely to break.

Your contract's language protects you when gear fails despite the backups. Make sure it includes:

  • A clause covering equipment failure and what remedy the client receives
  • A clause covering weather and rescheduling
  • A defined delivery window, not a promise of a specific date
  • A statement that you deliver edited images and retain RAW files

The post-event routine

The job isn't done when you leave.

  1. Ingest both cards to two separate drives before you sleep.
  2. Verify the file count matches the shot count on camera.
  3. Don't format the cards until the gallery is delivered and backed up in a third location.
  4. Log which gear misbehaved. Patterns show up over a season.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if your camera or flash breaks during my event?

Nothing changes for you. I carry two flashes and dual memory card slots on every job, plus a second camera body for full-day coverage like weddings. One piece of gear failing doesn't stop your coverage.

Do you bring backup equipment to every shoot?

Yes. Every event gets backup batteries, backup memory cards, and a backup flash trigger, no matter the length. Full-day events also get a second camera body, either owned or rented.

What if a memory card fails during my event?

My camera writes to two memory cards at once, so a single card failure never costs you images. I also don't format any card until your gallery is delivered and backed up in two separate places.

What happens if my event runs longer than expected and your batteries die?

I pack more batteries than the event should need. For a four-hour event I bring four camera batteries and two flash batteries, so a longer timeline doesn't leave you without coverage.

What does your contract say if equipment fails during my event?

My contract spells out what happens if equipment fails and what remedy you receive, along with a clause for weather and rescheduling. You'll know exactly where you stand before you book.

How soon will I get my photos, and are they backed up?

You get a defined delivery window in writing, not just a rough promise. Once I deliver your gallery, I keep it backed up in at least two separate locations along with the original RAW files.
