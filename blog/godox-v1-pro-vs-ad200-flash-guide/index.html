Godox V1 vs V1 Pro vs AD200 Pro II: What Actually Belongs in an Event Photographer's Bag

Compare the Godox V1, V1 Pro, and AD200 Pro II for events and portraits, including which flash to buy first and why the ecosystem matters.

July 5, 2026
6 min read

My original Godox V1 died mid-season. Not a dead battery, not a bad contact. The unit stopped firing and no amount of cleaning the hot shoe or reseating the trigger brought it back. That failure forced a decision I had been putting off, and it turned into the most useful gear research I have done all year.

Here is what I learned comparing the three flashes most event and portrait shooters end up choosing between.

The short answer

If you shoot events and portraits with one light, buy the V1 Pro.

If you already own a working speedlight and you want your portraits to look like someone lit them on purpose, buy the AD200 Pro II.

If you can only spend money once and you want the setup that covers everything, buy both. The V1 Pro handles receptions, ballrooms, and run-and-gun coverage. The AD200 Pro II handles anything where you have thirty seconds to place a light.

What the original V1 got right and where it fails

The V1 introduced the round head to the affordable market. Round heads matter because the falloff at the edge of the beam is smoother, so bounce light looks less like a rectangle bouncing off a ceiling. Skin renders softer. Shadow edges look less like a flash fired.

The original V1 has real problems though.

The magnetic modifier mount

The stock dome and the magnetic accessories work fine until you shove the flash in a bag between shots. Modifiers pop off. I lost a diffusion dome at a wedding reception and shot the rest of the night bouncing bare.

The battery and the recycle time

The V1 recycles in about 1.5 seconds at full power on a fresh battery. That is fine for portraits. During a first dance or a graduation walk it is not fine. You will miss frames.

The failure mode

Mine died. Search any Sony or Godox forum and you will find enough reports of dead V1 units to treat it as a known risk rather than bad luck. That matters when someone paid you to be there.

What the V1 Pro fixes

The V1 Pro is not a cosmetic refresh. Three changes matter for paid work.

A sub-flash on the front. When you bounce off a ceiling, the sub-flash throws a small amount of light straight forward. That kills the raccoon eyes you get from pure ceiling bounce. It is the single best reason to upgrade.

A modeling lamp that is actually usable. You can preview your light before you fire. For portraits in a dark venue this saves test frames and saves your subject's patience.

A locking modifier mount. The magnets got a mechanical lock. Accessories stay on.

The V1 Pro keeps the same 76Ws output, the same round head, and the same Godox 2.4GHz radio system. Nothing you already own becomes obsolete.

Where the AD200 Pro II wins

The AD200 Pro II is a different tool. It is a 200Ws strobe in a body you can hold in one hand, and it puts out roughly two and a half stops more light than a speedlight.

That extra output buys you two things.

You can overpower the sun. At noon in Charleston in July, a speedlight through a softbox at ten feet is losing. The AD200 wins that fight with power to spare.

You can use real modifiers. A 24 inch softbox or a beauty dish on a speedlight eats most of your output. On an AD200 you still have headroom.

The tradeoff is obvious. You need a stand, you need an assistant or a very cooperative client, and you cannot walk a reception with it on camera. It is a portrait light, not a coverage light.

Why the ecosystem decides this, not the flash

Every one of these units runs on the same Godox 2.4GHz radio. One X3 or Xpro trigger fires all of them. One set of modifiers with a Bowens or S2 bracket adapts across the line.

That is the real argument for staying inside Godox. The moment you buy a Profoto or a Sony flash, you are running two trigger systems and two modifier standards at the same event. Compatibility beats a marginal spec improvement every time.

What I run now

One V1 Pro on camera as the working light. One AD200 Pro II with a 32 inch octabox for portrait setups and outdoor fill. One X3 trigger. Extra batteries for both, because a dead light and no light are the same thing to a client.

The dead V1 stays in the bag as a rough backup for a season. It fires intermittently, which is worthless as a primary and occasionally useful as a fallback.

FAQ

Is the Godox V1 Pro worth upgrading to from the original V1?

Yes if you shoot bounce flash indoors. The sub-flash alone changes how ceiling bounce looks on faces. If you only use the flash off camera in a softbox, the upgrade matters less.

Can the Godox V1 Pro overpower the sun?

Not reliably. At 76Ws with a modifier attached you will struggle past f/5.6 at ISO 100 in direct midday light. Use it as fill instead, or bring the AD200 Pro II.

Does the AD200 Pro II work with Sony TTL and HSS?

Yes. Use the Sony version of the trigger, and HSS lets you shoot at 1/2000 and above so you can keep a wide aperture in daylight.

How many flashes do I need for a paid event?

Two. One working light and one backup. Single-point-of-failure gear is the most common reason a paid shoot goes sideways, and flashes fail more often than bodies or lenses.

Are third-party round head flashes as good as Profoto?

For color consistency shot to shot, no. For everything a portrait or event photographer actually delivers to a client, the gap does not show up in the final images.

What batteries should I buy?

Buy two spares per body. The V1 Pro uses a proprietary lithium-ion pack rated for roughly 480 full-power pops. Real-world event use with modeling lamp on cuts that down substantially.
