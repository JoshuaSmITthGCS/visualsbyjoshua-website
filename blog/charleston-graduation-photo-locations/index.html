12 Charleston Graduation Photo Locations, With the Best Time to Shoot Each One

A shot-tested guide to graduation photo locations in Charleston, SC, including light direction, crowd levels, parking, and permit rules for each spot.

Generic location lists rarely hold up once you actually try to shoot there. They tell you Rainbow Row is colorful. They do not tell you that the light hits it from behind between 10am and 2pm and that a tour group walks through every four minutes.

Most Charleston location lists are written by people who have never shot there.

Here is what I have learned shooting graduation and portrait sessions across the peninsula.

Downtown and the peninsula

1. The Cistern Yard, College of Charleston

The signature location for any College of Charleston grad. Oak canopy, white columns, Spanish moss.

Best time: 8am to 10am, or the last hour before sunset. Midday sun through the canopy makes dappled light that ruins skin tones.

Crowds: Heavy in May. Go early.

Note: The college restricts commercial shoots during event setup. Check before you promise a client the spot.

2. White Point Garden and The Battery

Oaks, harbor, cannons, and a wide open sky that acts like a giant softbox on an overcast day.

Best time: Golden hour, shooting east to west along the seawall.

Crowds: Moderate. Weekday mornings stay open.

Parking: Street parking on Murray Blvd fills by 10am.

3. Rainbow Row

Thirteen pastel houses on East Bay. Instantly recognizable, which is both the appeal and the problem.

Best time: 7am to 9am. The houses face east, so morning light hits them directly and the colors pop without flash.

Crowds: Constant after 9am.

Tip: Shoot from across the street at 75mm to compress the color blocks and to cut the sidewalk out of the frame.

4. Charleston City Market

Texture, brick, market stalls, real energy in the background.

Best time: Open shade under the market roof any time from 10am to 4pm.

Crowds: Very heavy. Use a wide aperture and long lens to blur the crowd into color.

Quieter downtown spots

5. Charleston County Courthouse steps

Columns, symmetry, a classic and formal look that works well for law and business grads.

Best time: Late afternoon when the building shades the steps.

Crowds: Low on weekends.

6. Philadelphia Alley

A narrow cobblestone alley off Queen Street. Old brick, ivy, no sky in frame.

Best time: Midday, because the walls block the harsh sun and you get soft directional light down the alley.

Crowds: Low.

This is my go-to when a session lands at noon. Read Flash as Fill in Harsh Midday Sun for the backup plan when you cannot find shade.

7. Waterfront Park and the Pineapple Fountain

Water, palmettos, the pier swings, and long sightlines down the pier.

Best time: Sunrise for empty frames, or golden hour for warm side light off the harbor.

Crowds: Heavy midday.

Note: The pier swings make an easy seated pose that solves awkward hands immediately.

8. Angel Oak Tree, Johns Island

A 400 year old live oak with branches that reach the ground.

Best time: Right at opening, 9am. The canopy is dense so you are shooting in shade all day.

Rules: The park bans commercial photography without a permit. Check the current City of Charleston park rules before you book a session there.

Beaches and outskirts

9. Folly Beach Pier

Wide open sky, salt spray, a strong leading line down the pier.

Best time: One hour before sunset for backlight off the water.

Watch for: Wind. Cap and gown tassels and long hair go sideways fast. Bring clips.

10. Sullivan's Island, Station 18 to 22

Emptier than Folly, dunes and sea oats, fewer people in the background.

Best time: Golden hour.

Parking: Residential street parking only and it is strictly enforced.

11. Boone Hall Plantation, Mount Pleasant

The oak allee is dramatic. It also carries history that some clients do not want in their graduation photos, so ask before you suggest it.

Best time: Late afternoon.

Rules: Paid photo permit required.

12. Old Village, Mount Pleasant

Pitt Street Bridge at sunset, quiet residential streets, low crowds, easy parking.

Best time: Last 45 minutes of daylight at the Pitt Street Bridge for water and sky.

How to sequence a 60 minute session

Pick two locations within a ten minute drive. Never three.

  • Minutes 0-10. Wide establishing shots, subject walking, get them warmed up.
  • Minutes 10-25. Solo portraits at the primary spot, three expressions and two angles per setup.
  • Minutes 25-35. Drive and reset.
  • Minutes 35-50. Second location, cap and gown detail shots, diploma, tassel, cap toss.
  • Minutes 50-60. Family and friend group shots. Do these last, because family always arrives late.

FAQ

Do I need a permit for graduation photos in Charleston?

For most public streets and sidewalks, no. City parks and private properties like Angel Oak and Boone Hall require permits for commercial photography. Rules change, so verify with the venue before the session.

When is the best month for Charleston graduation photos?

Late April and early May. By June the heat and humidity make cap and gown sessions miserable and the light gets harsher.

What time of day should I book a Charleston photo session?

Two hours after sunrise or the 90 minutes before sunset. Downtown in July, anything between 11am and 4pm needs shade or flash.

Where can I take graduation photos without crowds?

Philadelphia Alley, the Old Village in Mount Pleasant, Sullivan's Island beyond Station 20, and the Cistern Yard before 9am.

How long should a graduation session be?

Sixty minutes covers two locations and roughly 40 to 60 delivered images. Ninety minutes if you want family groups and an outfit change.

Does rain cancel a Charleston session?

Overcast is better light than full sun. Real rain gets rescheduled. The City Market roof and the covered walkways downtown work as a backup for light rain.
